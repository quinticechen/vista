Who lends to UK small businesses, and why each lender wins where it does

£68bn of bank lending and £18.3bn from non‑banks in 2025. The market has sorted itself by cost structure: big banks keep their own customers' standard loans, fintechs take the fast, thin‑file end, and challenger banks take big, complex, secured loans.

3Why each group wins where it does

Deciding a loan costs about the same whether it is £30k or £3m, so small loans only work with automated decisions. Once everyone can automate, the edge comes from the other three costs.

Big Five Challenger & specialist banks Non‑bank lenders
Cost of funds Current accounts: the cheapest money in the market Retail savings deposits No deposits: warehouse lines and institutional investors
Data Own current-account history plus decades of default data Collateral, valuations, relationship knowledge Open Banking, credit bureaus, platform sales data
Finding customers Already their bank Brokers and relationship managers Direct digital, brokers, embedded in platforms
Deciding Instant for existing customers on small loans Underwriters and credit committees Mostly automated (Funding Circle: 73% instant)
Where they win Standard loans to own customers Big, complex, property-backed Fast, thin‑file, declined elsewhere

Illustration (round numbers, not a measured cost): if a manual decision costs ~£1,500, a £30k loan at a 4% net margin earns £1,200 a year, so it loses money in year one; a £1m loan earns £40,000. BCG (2025): automated credit decisioning typically cuts end-to-end credit-process cost by 30–40%. BBB (2026): sub-£50k lending needs "scale and high levels of automation". CMA (2016): "access to financial and transactional data is a barrier to entry and expansion to SME lending."

How to read these numbers

  • Bank lending (£68bn) is gross new lending in 2025, excluding overdrafts and credit cards. The Big Five (£27bn) and challengers (£40bn) are rounded, so they add to £67bn.
  • Non‑bank lending (£18.3bn) is a separate series covering only the lenders BBB tracks. It excludes debt funds, and asset finance can appear in more than one source.
  • Decision modes are a judgement from public product information, not company disclosures. Group loan books include mortgages and are not SME-only.
